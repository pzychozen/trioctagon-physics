### 4.1 Upper/lower nearest-boundary identity follows the height sign at tau=0

The PLUS tau=0 lower arc G-_k is the reflection of G+_k in the centered height plane. Write a point p=(x,y,w) and an upper boundary point q=(u,v,t), where t>=h>0. For its reflected partner q^-=(u,v,-t),

    ||p-q^-||² - ||p-q||² = 4wt.

For w>0, taking infima over corresponding boundary points gives

    d(p,G-_k)² >= d(p,G+_k)² + 4hw.

Thus every lower candidate has a strictly closer upper partner. A globally nearest arc must be upper. For w<0 the statement reverses; for w=0 each corresponding upper/lower pair ties, though not necessarily all three gap indices.

This explains the hemisphere part of the nearest-label result without interpreting gaps as attracting or steering the readout. It does not determine which of the three labels wins within a level. Its assumptions include the mirror-paired tau=0 reference; it is not silently extended to the positive-tau twisted/dilated lower geometry.
