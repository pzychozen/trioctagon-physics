## Two exact corollaries, without a novelty claim

**1. Static point measurements cannot identify common phase.** Fix observer settings, placement and any of these static references. Write c=cos chi, s=sin chi. Common phase sends x to cx−sy and y to sx+cy, so

(cx−sy) cross (sx+cy) = (c²+s²) x cross y = C,

and norm(exp(i chi)Omega)=kappa. Hence M, Z and X are identical. Every deterministic measurement of that same X against the fixed geometry—including sphere distance, six arc distances, patch membership and directed-ray incidence—is consequently identical. Nevertheless D_i(exp(i chi)Omega_i)=cos chi D_i(Omega_i)+sin chi n_i cross D_i(Omega_i), so nonzero face vectors rotate in their own fixed planes. Therefore those point/geometry measurements cannot recover common phase or all changes of the decoded arrows. This extends the already established invariance to these measurements; it asserts no native temporal evolution or global phase quotient of F.

**2. Envelope-only change can alter the full Z direction.** Fix Omega and theta; put A=alpha K and b=beta. Then Z(E)=A E H+b C. Direct expansion gives

Z(E1) cross Z(E2) = A b (E1−E2) (H cross C).

If A b (E1−E2)≠0 and H cross C≠0, the two full vectors are nonparallel and nonzero, hence their centered placed directions differ for the fixed nonzero isotropic scale/rotation. The retained state-1 comparison satisfies this: direction changes by about 0.222623° even though only the envelope changes. This would disappear from a macro-only calculation; no chiral term was removed here. Parallel/zero degeneracies are not covered by the nondegenerate conclusion.

These are elementary consequences of accepted definitions, useful for interpretation. No new foundational theorem, motion law or independently validated physical mechanism is claimed.
