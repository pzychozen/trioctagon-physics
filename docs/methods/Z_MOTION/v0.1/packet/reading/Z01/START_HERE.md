# NCM-Z-01 v0.1 — discovery and review

The existing Z observation changes between the two saved states even with its observer frozen. In this example it moves closer to the fixed center. The scheduled observer also changes the point's apparent direction: its nearest upper opening changes from G+0 to G+1, while the frozen observer remains nearest G+0. That distinction comes from the existing formulas, without animating the shell.

All scheduled and frozen points remain inside the original close sphere and above the center. None is on any of the six central opening boundaries or finite host notch triangles. None of the outward rays through these points hits a finite notch triangle. These are position/direction comparisons, not evidence of passage or energy leaving an opening.

The useful surprise is that a change in the observer can change which opening appears closest. Also, the saved common-phase display probe rotates each face arrow in its own fixed tangent plane while leaving frozen staged Z unchanged, to rounding scale. Z alone cannot recover all the motion visible in those arrows.

Only updates 0 and 1 were retained: one displacement, no continuous path, turn between displacements, speed, period or flow. This is a useful reconciliation of existing definitions, with two elementary exact corollaries; no new physical mechanism or novelty claim.
