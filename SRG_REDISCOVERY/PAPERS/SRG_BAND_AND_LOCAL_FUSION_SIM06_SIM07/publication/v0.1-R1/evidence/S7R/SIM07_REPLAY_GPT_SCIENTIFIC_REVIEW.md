## 6. Additional comparison: observer groups and interaction groups are not interchangeable

The graph covers incoming boundaries 0–199, supplying updates 1–200. No new boundary-200 graph or update 201 was computed here. Comparisons below align both definitions at the same saved boundary.

At **boundary 199**, the spatial observer has ten groups, while the local selection graph has **13 weak** and **14 strong** components. Weak means connected after ignoring direction; strong requires directed paths both ways. In particular:

- The observer's single P1 group of 20 points contains three weak graph components of **8, 6 and 6**.
- The observer's single P3 group of 20 contains two weak components of **12 and 8**.

It is not always merely a matter of the observer giving a coarser partition. At that same boundary, P1 is one observer group but three graph components. In this recorded configuration, the two partitions therefore cross rather than having one universal finer/coarser relationship.

This is an additional passive interpretation of the existing partitions, not a new model or a change to the observer. It clarifies why neither ten groups nor six initial populations alone determines interaction structure.

## 8. Ordered fusion is verified at actual operands

For every captured row i, its neighbor selections remain those computed from the incoming state. If selected j < i, the actual coordinate read equals j's recorded post-fusion row. If j > i, the read equals j's recorded post-local coordinate Y. All **120,000** selected-coordinate reads satisfy that identity. Self selections are absent. Each row's own pre-write position equals Y_i; Q is fixed during the stage.

Over all passes, **60,118** neighbor-read visits use earlier written rows and **59,882** use later unwritten rows. The weighted contribution of earlier coordinate changes is nonzero in **19,974 / 24,000** recorded centers. This is a count of actual operands and their reporting decomposition, not a numerical experiment with a different update order.

At **pass 16, row 6**, selected neighbors are **2, 5, 11, 16, 18**. Rows 2 and 5 have already moved; the other three have not. In particular, ID 5's post-local value is approximately

`(10.1642402234, -0.009263063370, -1.0188735915)`

while row 6 actually reads

`(10.1645760721, -0.008571991931, -1.0211011336)`.

For normalized weights a_j and actual reads z_j, the reporting quantity

`d_i = sum_j a_j (z_j - Y_j)`

accounts for earlier changes in the one actual center. At the three supplied examples its norm is:

- pass 1, row 112: **0.008262981568**;
- pass 16, row 6: **0.001045505511**;
- pass 200, row 8: **0.0000213994194**.

The review checks the recorded center and write against the original formulas without evolving any alternative coordinate array. It establishes that earlier writes truly enter later calculations, but not the final-state difference a simultaneous or reverse-order model would produce. No such control is required to accept this reconstruction.

## 9. Compression and the old spatial-spread statistic

The smallest observed log-coordinate distance to either bound is approximately **3.032068826**, not a marginal threshold contact. The prescribed L still oscillates, and the original log/exp expression still executes. This finite record does not establish general irrelevance of breathing or explain a different driver's static/breathing comparison. No breathing-off run was performed.

The captured coordinates also make the older spread-definition distinction tangible. For P3 at pass 200, the original pooled-coordinate `np.std(pos_k)` is **3.238809712**, whereas true vector-centered radius of gyration is **0.066949097** and diameter is **0.166308988**. The previously accepted exact decomposition verifies at all six endpoint populations. The old output is preserved, not overwritten with the newer measurement.

This is source-and-record explanation, not a physical condensate, ground-state, conservation-law or infinite-time result. See [F05 metrics/amplitudes](source_package/figures/F05_metrics_and_amplitudes.png).
