\chapter*{Reader's orientation}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Reader's orientation}
Hilmir, these programs begin with a small number of positions and velocities.
Their shapes and trails are ways of showing those values. The most useful
reconstruction therefore follows two histories at once: what the arrays do,
and what the recording and drawing code lets us see.

The first program moves three points using their pairwise directions. Its
surviving historical picture is a completed drawing, not a movie. A separately
authorized seed-0 reproduction supplies a detailed chronology: two points
nearly meet and then separate, while another moves in the opposite direction
along much the same visible diagonal. The reproduction clarifies how this rule
can make overlapping trails, but does not recover the lost run behind the old
picture.

The second program moves eight centers in three dimensions toward a
sequentially recalculated group average. Each center is decorated with a fixed
tetrahedron. Its movie survives, but its numerical history does not. We can
describe specific visible relocations and explain the source mechanisms that
permit them without inventing unseen coordinates.

The third program retains that centroid construction and adds a binary response
switch. Its two original logs reveal what the movie alone cannot: a switch can
first slow a center before reversing its movement; frame labels can restart
while the numerical state continues; and a large displayed coordinate jump
can accompany a small native step. Its “teleport” diagnostic has a modulo
defect that flags many ordinary negative coordinate changes.

This volume connects those findings to exact source statements, equations,
rows and images. It preserves unresolved provenance, including the strong but
formally unlinked correspondence between the third movie and its shorter log.
It completes a first volume of reconstruction, not the wider SRG project.

\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}
We reconstruct three historical SRG moving-center programs:
\filepath{recursive_attractor.py}, \filepath{3Dtriangles.py}, and
\filepath{plot_1.py}. The account integrates complete source construction,
accepted inspection records, attributed historical graphics and movies, one
previously authorized fixed-seed reproduction, and original eight-body CSV
evidence. Exact transcriptions preserve sequential update order, wrapped
coordinates, carried velocities, a prescribed damped oscillatory gain,
componentwise logarithmic damping, and, where present, a mean-speed response
factor and stochastic binary interaction sign.

The three-point reproduction establishes a finite near-contact followed by
separation, not a recovered historical path-inheritance episode. The
no-switch tetrahedral movie supports visible relocation and slower late
movement, while lacking the numerical record required for exact displacement
claims. The switching program's logs contain 1,092 and 440 eight-body callback
blocks despite 200 nominal movie labels. They preserve repeated labels and
state continuity through clock resets. At checkable transitions, native
increments remain below 0.062 coordinate units; actual coordinate seam events
number 22 and 7, whereas the source diagnostic would flag 8,151 and 3,129 body
events. The signed contribution can change before velocity reverses.

We derive the source-level drive--damping bookkeeping identity for the recorded
kinetic quantity and distinguish it from physical energy conservation. The
central conclusion is methodological and mathematical: update ordering,
retained state, clock semantics and measurement conventions are necessary to
interpret apparent collective behavior. Source-associated media, reproduced
data, derived identities and unresolved interpretations remain separate.
