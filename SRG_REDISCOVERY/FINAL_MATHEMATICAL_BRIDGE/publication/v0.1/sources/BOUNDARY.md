## 3. Claim group I — the single-lens response

### 3.1 Explicit map and conditional uniqueness

Reuse the established lens area:

    A_lens(theta) = R_L^2 (2 theta - sin(2 theta)).

Define the admitted area fraction and normalized balanced channel vector:

    I(theta) = A_lens(theta)/(pi R_L^2)
             = (2 theta - sin(2 theta))/pi,
    f0 = (1,1,1)^T / sqrt(3).

The proposed preparation is

    P_area(theta, xi) = sqrt(I(theta)) [xi tensor f0].                 (C1)

The tensor order is helicity-major, C^2 tensor C^3.

**Proof from the stated response assumptions.** A2–A3 specify a scalar response multiplying xi tensor f0, with gain a(theta)>=0. Since ||f0||=1, its squared norm is a(theta)^2 ||xi||^2. A1 requires this to equal I(theta)||xi||^2 for every xi. Thus a(theta)^2=I(theta) and the nonnegative convention gives a(theta)=sqrt(I(theta)). A4 supplies the zero value at theta=0. Conversely, (C1) satisfies each stated assumption. This is a unique response in this specified class, not a proof that geometry alone selects this class.

### 3.2 Domains, information and symmetry

Direct differentiation gives

    I(0)=0, I(pi/2)=1, I'(theta)=(4/pi) sin^2(theta).

Thus I is continuous, strictly increasing on [0,pi/2], and lies in [0,1]. In particular:

    ||P_area(theta,xi)||^2 = I(theta)||xi||^2,
    ||P_area(theta,xi)|| <= ||xi||.

For a fixed theta this preparation is complex-linear in the *incident amplitude* xi, not in the lens separation. It can accept either helicity sector or their superposition.

Let X cyclically permute the channels, p=f0 f0^dagger, and Pi_core=I_2 tensor p. Then

    (I_2 tensor X) P_area = P_area,
    Pi_core P_area = P_area.

These follow from the **explicit equal coherent response**, not from uniform intensities alone. P_area(theta,conj(xi))=conj(P_area(theta,xi)) in the chosen real channel frame, so the boundary itself does not impose channel chirality.

At coincidence, P_area(pi/2,xi)=xi tensor f0. At tangency, P_area(0,xi)=0. For fixed nonzero xi the raw norm determines theta uniquely, because I is strictly increasing. This is lost if one only retains the normalized corridor ray. Unknown incident magnitude introduces the expected amplitude/aperture ambiguity; the map does not estimate both from one norm.

The normalization by pi R_L^2 makes the rule scale-independent for fixed xi and fixed d/R_L. That is a chosen toy convention. It is not evidence that physical response must discard absolute radius or that xi cannot itself depend on radius in a later incident-field model.

### 3.3 Regularity and the zero endpoint

Taylor expansion at theta=0 gives

    I(theta) = (4/(3 pi)) theta^3 - (4/(15 pi)) theta^5 + O(theta^7),
    sqrt(I(theta)) = 2/sqrt(3 pi) theta^(3/2) [1 - theta^2/10 + O(theta^4)].

For fixed xi the response has a continuous one-sided derivative at zero, equal to zero, and is C^1 in the aperture parameter on the closed interval (with one-sided endpoint derivatives). It is not generally C^2 at zero for nonzero xi.

For x=d/(2R_L) approaching 1 from below, theta~sqrt(2(1-x)), so

    sqrt(I) ~ sqrt(8 sqrt(2)/(3 pi)) (1-x)^(3/4).

Consequently, a claim of a uniformly bounded response derivative with respect to separation at tangency would be false. The response is continuous there but not locally Lipschitz in x for a fixed nonzero input. This is a stated sensitivity feature, not a reason to hide the endpoint or introduce epsilon-normalization.

For stable floating evaluation use the same small-angle expression, not subtraction of nearly equal numbers. The supplied candidate evaluates the Taylor sum through degree 13 when theta<0.1. For x=2theta<0.2 the next alternating term bounds the exact truncation error in the area fraction by x^15/(15! pi). This bound is for truncation, not a machine-roundoff certificate. Positive-area floating underflow is signalled explicitly.
