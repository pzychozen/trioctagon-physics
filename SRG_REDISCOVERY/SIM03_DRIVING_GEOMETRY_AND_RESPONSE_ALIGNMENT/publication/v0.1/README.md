# Driving, Geometry, and Response Alignment in Early SRG Motion

*An Exact Observed-Path Decomposition of Repeated Forcing and Nonlinear Damping*  
Hilmir Frímann Halldórsson · v0.1 · 14 October 2026

Repeated driving can renew similar speed envelopes while labeled configurations continue to change. This paper explains the accepted SIM03 result by separating impulse magnitude, signed velocity–impulse alignment and damping in both a squared-speed ledger and an exact ledger for actual mean speed.

The geometry's distance factor is usually near one, with measured exceptions. Alignment remains substantial and can oppose incoming motion. Damping reduces fixed pre-damping vector differences; its separately measured reduction of scalar-speed variance occurs at all 200 phases in both cycle views. The paper distinguishes those observed facts from a theorem about the full state update.

Two subsequent reviewer deductions sharpen the interpretation: the box and regularizer make q ≥ 0.999 impossible, and near-equal incoming/outgoing phase-averaged spreads largely reflect one-step reindexing. Neither is evidence of universal convergence or learned memory.
