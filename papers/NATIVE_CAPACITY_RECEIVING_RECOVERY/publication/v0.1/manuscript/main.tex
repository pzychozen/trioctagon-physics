\begin{abstract}
Finite-time access to the joint projection capacity of the reciprocal chiral exchange candidate does not by itself determine receiver allocation or later native recovery. We establish both on the same unchanged solution for the coherent empty launch $\Omega_0=(1,e^{i\phi},1)$, $0<\phi\le1/100$ radians, at yaw $\pi/4$. Fix finite positive coefficients before phase so that $\omega_R=\nu/3$ and $0<\gamma/\nu\le1/100$, with the rates defined in the original variables below. The admitted periodic carrier retains positive propagation and disjoint finite-width $H^1$ profiles. Writing $C=\alpha_N(1-\cos\phi)$ for the joint capacity and $\varpi=\sqrt{17/18}\,\nu$, the episode satisfies
\[
\begin{gathered}
 E_{\rm out}(1/\nu)>\frac{1053}{4096}C,\qquad
 H_R(\pi/\varpi)>C/4,\\
 H_C(\pi/\varpi)<C/100,\qquad
 E_{\rm out}(2\pi/\varpi)<C/100 .
\end{gathered}
\]
The first bound is inherited after checking the same coefficients. Net native recovery to $2\pi/\varpi$ exceeds $24/25$ of each actual intermediate external stock, at $1/\nu$ and $\pi/\varpi$, respectively. A complete moving-native tangent reference and a uniform finite error below $\sqrt{2C}/10$ prove the new stock bounds. The normalization is joint capacity, not the whole initial native store; absolute capacity tends to zero with phase. The equality-constrained sufficient family proves neither saturation, irreversible retention, monotonic recovery, an exact nonlinear period nor a spatial circuit.
\end{abstract}
