\section{From accessible capacity to receiving and recovery}\label{sec:introduction}
The reciprocal chiral exchange candidate couples an evolving native state to a periodic carrier and a conservative collocated receiver~\cite{p1}. Its joint projection ceiling bounds the trial storage that can leave the native state~\cite{p3}. The finite-capacity access theorem then supplies an actual external stock larger than a quarter of that ceiling at an explicit finite time~\cite{p4}. That access result leaves two separate questions: how much of the stock reaches the receiver, and whether the native store subsequently recovers a quantified amount on the same solution.

Earlier receiving and recovery guarantees use a different sufficient coefficient regime~\cite{p2,p3}. Section~\ref{sec:compatibility} shows why those certificates cannot simply be appended to the access theorem. We instead use a tangent comparison in which the native mode, carrier and receiver all move. Its full finite-error estimate controls the exact nonlinear solution with positive propagation throughout the episode. This paper presents the accepted receiving/recovery investigation~\cite{investigation,review}; it introduces no new model, parameter optimization or physical interpretation.

The result is deliberately restricted. Coefficients are fixed before every phase in a small interval, and the relation between native and receiver rates is an equality. The theorem is a certificate for this nonempty subfamily, not an open robustness neighborhood or a classification of all couplings. \emph{Capacity-sized} always refers to the joint ceiling, which itself becomes small near the aligned launch.

For clarity, the four predecessor results with the same theorem number have distinct roles. P1 Theorem~3.1 supplies global existence and strong empty-launch regularity; P2 Theorem~3.1 is the older native-credit result; P3 Theorem~3.1 supplies projection capacity; and P4 Theorem~3.1 supplies finite-time access. Hereafter P1--P4 mean references~\cite{p1,p2,p3,p4}, respectively.
