Abstract
On an accepted invariant coherent domain of the native Tri-Octagon map, ﬁve real re-
tained coordinates determine the full complex state up to common phase. We recover the
complete native history from their closed evolution and one initial common phase. A canoni-
cal representative gives an explicit unit multiplier and a continuous ordinary increment with
absolute-summability control and the inherited limiting-phase bound. The adjacent-state
overlap is retained-data dependent and nonvanishing, but its angle diﬀers from the canon-
ical increment by the angle of the inner product of consecutive representatives. We prove
the branch-resolved correction, its accumulation rule and its change under a diﬀerent repre-
sentative; an analytic family shows that the correction is not identically zero. Composing
reconstruction with the adopted lossless face decoder yields exact vector histories. Pure
rephasing rotates each face vector about its ﬁxed normal line, and those positioned axes co-
incide with the existing stationary support lines, with reversed directed-angle convention. In
contrast, a single proper ambient rotation representing that action for every admitted state
and every ﬁxed channel label exists only for whole-turn phases. The complete native step
can also change amplitudes and relative phases. We separate these results from staged obser-
vations, memory updates and observations from current memory, and from any assignment
of a material feature or rigid-body pose.

1 Question and scope
The retained coordinates of the coherent triad distinguish the component amplitudes and relative
phases, but discard a common phase. Relative phase describes how the channels diﬀer from one
another. Common phase rotates all three complex components together while preserving their
pair coherences. Once the original common phase is supplied, the existing native map determines
its subsequent evolution; there is no additional phase law to choose.
This distinction becomes spatial through the adopted face decoder. Each complex channel
supplies the two coeﬃcients of an arrow in a ﬁxed tangent plane. A common complex rephasing
rotates these arrows in their respective planes. Whether the resulting axes coincide with existing
stationary supports, and whether the three vector changes form one common proper ambient
rotation, are precise questions about the adopted deﬁnitions. They do not require a material-
motion interpretation.
The published predecessor establishes the retained coordinates, their exact image, autonomous
evolution and energy retention [ 1]. We combine the accepted phase and spatial investigations
[4, 5]: exact state reconstruction, the nontrivial overlap-angle correction, positioned-axis cor-
respondence, and a quantiﬁed proper-rotation test. Their continuous-domain arguments are
presented here. The inherited invariant-domain and convergence results [ 2], decoder [ 3], ﬁxed-
gap geometry [ 6], and observation deﬁnitions [ 7] retain their own hypotheses. No unrestricted
coordinate-minimality or external priority claim is made.
