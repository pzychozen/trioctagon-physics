## S02 — uniform enclosure of each individual hull

Let \(\mathcal H_k^\sigma=\operatorname{conv}(G_k^\sigma)\). Since each G consists of two joined segments containing all three listed vertices, its convex hull is exactly their closed triangle: the segments are contained in the triangle, and every convex set containing G contains its vertices and their convex combinations. This is a bounding set only. The proof does not add its missing edge or interior to the physical model.

On the horizontal plane J^T=−J and J²=−I. Writing b=τ/r_g,

\[
A_\tau=\begin{pmatrix}1&b\\-b&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
A_\tau^TA_\tau=(1+b^2)I,
\quad A_\tau=\sqrt{1+b^2}R_{-\arctan b}. \tag{V8}
\]

The norm multiplier is exact. Since 0≤τ≤h_g, it is at most

\[
D_*:=\sqrt{1+(h_g/r_g)^2}
=\sqrt{1+3(\sqrt2-1)^2}=\sqrt{10-6\sqrt2}. \tag{V9}

D_*≥1 also bounds the unchanged upper coordinates. Hence each ring elbow has ρ≤D_*a_g at |ζ|=h_g, and each tip has ρ≤D_*r_g at |ζ|=H_0. S preserves ρ and |ζ|, so the same vertex bounds apply to MINUS.

Every individual upper hull has h_g≤ζ≤H_0, every lower one −H_0≤ζ≤−h_g. Thus the entire hull stays in one halfspace on which this convexity argument is valid.

## S03 — separation, true Euclidean clearance and scaling

From (V5),

\[
\frac{K_c}{R_c}=\frac{2+\sqrt3}{\sqrt7}>1
\]

because (2+√3)²=7+4√3>7. The signed horizontal coefficient K_c+R_c cosθ is therefore strictly positive at every θ. Since each u_j is a horizontal unit vector, the radius and height are exactly

\[
\rho(\Gamma_j(\theta))=K_c+R_c\cos\theta,\qquad
\zeta(\Gamma_j(\theta))=R_c\sin\theta. \tag{V13}
\]

This step removes a possible absolute-value ambiguity in cylindrical radius. It is uniform in j. Define L_*=√(1+(3/5)²)=√34/5. Cauchy–Schwarz applied to the unit vector (cosθ,|sinθ|) gives

\[
\cos\theta-\frac35|\sin\theta|\ge-L_*,\quad
\Phi(\Gamma_j(\theta))\ge K_c-L_*R_c,
\quad \Delta_*:=K_c-L_*R_c-\frac3{50}. \tag{V14}
\]

The trigonometric minimum is attained when cosθ=−1/L_* and |sinθ|=(3/5)/L_*, but this does not imply that a closest hull point also saturates its enclosure. In particular the resulting distance estimate need not be an actual set-to-set minimum.

Derive all coarse constants from (V10), not decimal evaluations. Since R_c=√7K_c/(2+√3),

\[
K_c>\frac{45401}{60000}>\frac{756}{1000},\qquad
K_c<\frac{45403}{60000},\qquad
R_c<\frac{600636287}{1119600000}<\frac{537}{1000}.
\]

The last rational gap is 588913/1119600000>0. Positivity of the denominator justifies taking its lower bound when bounding R_c above; correlated radicals cause no problem for a valid interval enclosure. Also (1167/1000)²−34/25=1889/1000000>0, so L_*<1167/1000. It follows strictly that

\[
\Delta_*>\frac{756}{1000}
-\frac{1167}{1000}\frac{537}{1000}-\frac3{50}
=\frac{69321}{1000000}>0. \tag{V15}
\]

Thus every circle point P and every hull point Q satisfy Φ(P)−Φ(Q)≥Δ_*>0. This already excludes intersection, but Δ_* is a scalar-function gap, not yet a Euclidean distance.

\[
\begin{aligned}

&\le |\rho(P)-\rho(Q)|+\frac35\big||\zeta(P)|-|\zeta(Q)|\big|\\

\[
\frac{69321}{1000000}-\frac{297}{5000}\frac{1167}{1000}

In particular,

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{dist}(\Gamma_j,\mathcal H_k^\sigma)
\ge\frac{\Delta_*}{L_*}
>\frac{69321/1000000}{1167/1000}
=\frac{23107}{389000}>\frac{297}{5000}.} \tag{V17}

All strict comparisons divide positive quantities.
