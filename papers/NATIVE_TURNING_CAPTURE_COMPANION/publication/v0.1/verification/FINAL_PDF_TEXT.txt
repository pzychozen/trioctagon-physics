Abstract
The original collective-turning example of the native triad was known to have a strict
early turn, while a separate theorem established coherent relaxation for states in an invariant
annulus and ordinary phase sector. We connect these results for the unchanged recurrence,
rational coeﬃcients and original exact entrance. A retained certiﬁcate encloses 50 full updates
in one connected chain, starting from an exact singleton. A separate exact-fraction checker
veriﬁes the chain, nonzero prestages and strict terminal annulus and cone inequalities. These
imply inclusion in the inherited coherent domain at update 50, a suﬃcient index rather
than a certiﬁed earliest entry. The inherited theorem then gives convergence of the same
turning trajectory to its positive unequal amplitude proﬁle, up to a limiting common phase.
Continuity also supplies an open neighborhood of entrances with both properties at the same
coeﬃcients, without an explicit radius. No parameter robustness, global basin or physical
identiﬁcation is asserted.
1 The missing ﬁnite connection
The parent paper [ 1] established two distinct results at one native parameter point: a certiﬁed
nonzero turn of the complex channel mean over its ﬁrst two increments, and convergence for
states already admitted to an explicit coherent domain. The turning entrance had amplitudes
outside that domain. Accordingly, the earlier edition did not assert that its particular turning
trajectory eventually entered the convergence region.
The accepted investigation [ 2, 4] supplies that missing ﬁnite link. For the original seed, its
unchanged full evolution is strictly inside the same domain at update 50. This is a supplementary
result: the parent paper’s statement of the then-open gap was accurate, and the earlier edition
remains unchanged.
The contribution is the certiﬁed ﬁnite connection and its qualitative initial-condition corollary.
The early turn, invariant domain, convergence estimates and limiting proﬁle are inherited. Nei-
ther interval certiﬁcation nor the implication from ﬁnite entry to an invariant convergence the-
orem is claimed as a new method. The accepted prior-source comparison found a distinct gap
within the inspected project corpus; it is not a worldwide novelty claim or an additional mathe-
matical theorem.
The argument below is readable without executing the evidence archive. We state the exact
object, explain the ﬁnite enclosure induction and its terminal tests, and apply the inherited
theorem at the correct index. Full rational endpoints and the historical execution records remain
available as supporting evidence.

3.2 Two arithmetic organizations
The generator uses integer endpoints divided by S = 2 512. Rational constants are rounded down
and up. Products take the extreme endpoint products and round outward; positive reciprocals
reverse the endpoints before outward division. Squares include zero if appropriate, and square
roots use integer square-root bounds. The documented padding only widens boxes. Its purpose
does not supply proof by itself: the separate checker must verify the required containments.
The checker uses exact fractions rather than importing the generator’s dyadic arithmetic. It
evaluates the prestage in the combined form

6 Nearby entrances at the same coeﬃcients
Corollary 6.1 (Qualitative initial-condition neighborhood) . At the ﬁxed coeﬃcients (2.4), there
is an open neighborhood U ⊂ C3 of (2.5) such that every entrance in U has Km > 0 for its ﬁrst
two mean increments and belongs to int D at update 50. Every such trajectory converges to the
same amplitude proﬁle r∗, up to an entrance-dependent limiting common phase.
Proof. Identify C3 with R6. Every prestage on the certiﬁed ﬁnite orbit is nonzero, with the strict
lower bound in Theorem 3.1. Near each visited state, the polynomial prestage and normalized-
product expression ( 3.2) give a smooth real representation of the native map. Positive moduli
permit the square roots and reciprocal operations. Agreement with the native phase rule holds
even across a principal-argument cut.
Because only ﬁnitely many updates are involved, repeated continuity gives an open initial neigh-
borhood V of Ω0 on which all prestages through update 49 remain nonzero and F 50 is continuous.
On V , the ﬁrst two mean increments and their statistic Km are continuous. Equation ( 2.7) is
strict at Ω0, so {z ∈ V : Km(z) > 0} is an open neighborhood of that seed.

U = V ∩ {z : Km(z) > 0} ∩ (F 50|V )−1(O) (6.1)
is open and contains Ω0. The same U satisﬁes ﬁnite-path regularity, strict early turning and

The result supplies some positive-radius neighborhood by openness, but no numerical radius or
uniform quantitative perturbation allowance. Coeﬃcients remain ﬁxed. It does not establish
parameter robustness, a complete basin, stability of an earliest-entry index or identical limiting
phases throughout U .
7 Interpretation and limits
The ﬁnite certiﬁcate connects two known behaviors along one unchanged native evolution: the
documented early turn is followed by entry into the known aligned regime. It does not attribute
capture to one term based on a single history. In the inherited exact mean accounting, mn+1 −
mn = Bn +Sn, the onsite contribution is Bn = (ε/3) ∑ i Ωi,n(ki −|Ωi,n|2) and the synchronization
contribution is Sn = (1 /3) ∑ i vi,n(eiδi,n − 1). Graph corrections cancel directly in the prestage
mean but change the individual states supplied to later operations [ 1, eq:budget].
The parent’s delayed-graph comparison uses λ = 0; it is not an ablation establishing a sole cause
of the present nonzero- λ capture. No physical position, force, gravity, magnetism, receiver,
transport channel, clock or moving-shell law follows from this note. Nor does convergence prove
that only ﬁnitely many diminishing directional changes remain. The certiﬁed index is suﬃcient
rather than earliest, and the qualitative neighborhood is not a global attraction claim.
