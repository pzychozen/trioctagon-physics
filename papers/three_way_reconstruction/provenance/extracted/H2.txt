Phase 1 — Introduction and Motivation
The concept of infinity has traditionally been treated as a binary distinction: a quantity
is either finite or divergent. Classical mathematics, since the work of Georg Cantor, has
shown that infinities may differ in cardinality, distinguishing between the countable and
the uncountable. Yet, in analytic and physical systems, infinity seldom appears as an
abstract set; it arises through the continuous growth or curvature of a function. This
observation inspired the development of theThree-Way Resonance Model (Three-
way)and its derivative, theReciprocal Transformation Method (RTM). Together,
they recast divergence not as the breakdown of mathematics, but as a form of resonance
geometry that can be measured and compared.
The central motivation of this work is to formalize that geometric intuition: differ-
ent mathematical constants, when placed in identical dynamical environments, exhibit
distinct modes of divergence. Rather than viewing infinity as a monolithic endpoint, we
seek to treat it as aspectrum of curvature magnitudes—quantities that can be analyzed,
compared, and even contained.

resonance: a constant or parameter interacts simultaneously with two complementary

amplitude or geometric potential, (2) temporal or phase modulation, and (3) reflective
reciprocity. This triadic coupling produces a unique form of self-referential balance that
