Abstract
We present a controlled numerical investigation of a minimal family of reciprocal rational
functions exhibiting intrinsic three-way intersection points defined by the conditionf(x) =
±1. These intersections arise directly from the algebraic structure of the family and do not
depend on externally imposed symmetry. The central question addressed is whether such three-
way structures behave as fragile algebraic coincidences or as stable geometric features under
perturbation.
To evaluate this, we develop a reproducible experimental pipeline combining deterministic
perturbation scans across more than one thousand orders of magnitude, Monte Carlo ensemble
sampling with paired contrasts, robustness tests under injected noise, and dual-backend compu-
tation using both double precision and arbitrary precision arithmetic. We introduce diagnostic
observables designed to probe the sensitivity of the lock geometry under controlled parameter
perturbations.
Across all tested regimes, the three-way intersection exhibits smooth power-law sensitivity
scaling, reproducible detectability thresholds nearδ≈10 −12–10−13, and strong robustness un-
der perturbations, with qualitatively identical behavior across multiple perturbation protocols.
These findings are difficult to reconcile with numerical artifact explanations and instead support
the interpretation that the three-way intersection represents a genuine structural feature of the
construct.
The study does not propose a closed-form analytical theory, but establishes a rigorous nu-
merical foundation for further mathematical investigation. More broadly, it demonstrates how
carefully designed numerical experimentation can serve as a legitimate exploratory tool in the
study of novel geometric structures.

1 Introduction
Geometric structures defined by reciprocal transformations arise naturally across mathematics,
from projective dualities to rational maps and fixed-point theory. In certain families of such trans-
formations, one encounters distinguished intersection conditions where a map, its reciprocal, and
associated level sets coincide. These intersections are not imposed externally but emerge from the
algebraic structure of the family itself.
In this work we investigate a minimal example of this phenomenon: a family of reciprocal
rational functions exhibiting three-way intersection points characterized by the conditionf(x) =
±1. These points act as intrinsic fixed structures of the construct, independent of interpretation.
Our focus is not on proposing a physical theory, but on addressing a narrower and well-defined
question:
Does this three-way reciprocal structure behave as a stable geometric object under perturbation,
or is it merely a fragile algebraic coincidence?
To answer this, we adopt a numerical experimental approach. We define a controlled perturba-
tion protocol on the defining constants of the construct, introduce quantitative observables designed
to probe structural sensitivity, and evaluate behavior across several regimes:
•deterministic perturbation scans across more than 1000 orders of magnitude,
•Monte Carlo ensembles with paired contrasts,
•robustness tests under injected noise,
•and dual numerical backends (double precision and arbitrary precision).
The goal of this methodology is not to confirm a preconceived outcome, but to distinguish
between three possibilities:
1. the structure is numerically unstable and collapses under perturbation,
2. the structure survives but only as a numerical artifact of limited precision,
3. the structure exhibits smooth, reproducible behavior indicative of intrinsic stability.
Our results consistently support the third case. Across all tested regimes, the three-way in-
tersection exhibits smooth sensitivity scaling, clear detectability thresholds, and strong robustness
under perturbation. These findings suggest that the behavior observed is not accidental, but reflects
genuine structural properties of the underlying construct.
The remainder of the paper introduces the minimal rational family under study, details the
experimental pipeline, presents quantitative results, and discusses both the implications and limi-
tations of this numerical investigation.

2.3 Choice of Constants
Throughout this study we use the pair (A, B) = (π, e) as a representative transcendental example.

3 Experimental Methodology
To evaluate whether the three-way lock points defined in Section 2 represent structurally stable
features or fragile numerical coincidences, we constructed a controlled numerical experiment. The
methodology is designed to distinguish genuine geometric behavior from artifacts of finite precision,
noise, or implementation choices.
3.1 Perturbation Design
Let the baseline parameters be denoted
(A0, B0) = (π, e),
and letδ >0 be a small perturbation scale.
We define two parameter pairs for each trial:
•thespecialconfiguration: (A sp, Bsp),
•thecontrolconfiguration: (A ct, Bct),
and evaluate contrasts between observables computed from these two closely related systems.
Two perturbation protocols were implemented:
1.Single-parameter perturbation(b only):
(Act, Bct) = (A0, B0 +δ).
2.Antisymmetric perturbation(antisym):
(Act, Bct) = (A0 +δ, B 0 −δ).
These protocols are not modifications of the underlying construct; they are experimental probes
used to explore sensitivity along different directions in parameter space.

3.2 Lock Identification and Observables
For each parameter pair, numerical solutions to the equations
f(x;A, B) =±1
are computed, yielding two branches of lock points (denoted “plus” and “minus”) with solutions
nearx≈ ±1.
From these solutions we define two diagnostic observables:
•Astiffness contrast, denoted ∆ logS, defined as the difference in a logarithmic stability proxy
between the special and control systems.
•Adrift contrast, denoted ∆D, defined as the difference in deviation from unit modulus between
the corresponding lock points.
The precise algebraic definitions of these quantities are implementation-dependent and are pro-
vided in the accompanying code. Their purpose is not to define fundamental invariants, but to
serve as sensitive numerical probes of how the lock geometry responds to small parameter changes.
3.3 Deterministic Scans
To characterize the intrinsic scaling behavior of the construct, we perform deterministic scans over
perturbation magnitudes of the form
δ= 10 −k, k= 1,2, . . . ,1000.
For each value ofk, observables are computed once (without random noise) and the resulting
sensitivity curves are analyzed. This provides a direct view of the analytic behavior of the system
across more than one thousand orders of magnitude.
3.4 Monte Carlo Ensembles
To assess robustness and statistical distinguishability, we perform ensemble experiments withR
independent replicates (typicallyR= 4096). For each replicate, small Gaussian perturbations are
added to both special and control parameters using paired noise:
A→A+η A, B→B+η B, η A, ηB ∼ N(0, σ 2),
with the same noise realization applied to both special and control systems. This paired design
suppresses spurious noise while preserving true structural differences.
For eachδ, we compute:
•the ensemble mean of each contrast,
•95% confidence intervals,
•a detectability score measuring the fraction of replicates in which the sign of the contrast is
preserved.

3.5 Numerical Precision and Dual Backend
Because the experiments probe extremely small perturbations, a dual numerical backend was em-
ployed:
•standard double precision (float64) for moderateδ,
•arbitrary precision arithmetic (mpmath) for ultra-smallδ.
The backend is selected automatically based on perturbation magnitude, ensuring that the
observed scaling behavior is not an artifact of floating-point underflow.
3.6 Design Principle
The overall experimental pipeline is constructed around a single guiding principle:
If the three-way lock is a genuine structural feature of the construct, it should manifest
smooth scaling, reproducibility, and robustness across numerical regimes and perturba-
tion protocols.
The following sections present the results of these experiments.

Monte Carlo ensemble experiments (withR= 4096) provide a statistical perspective on when the

4.3 Robustness Under Noise
To test whether the observed behavior is fragile under perturbations, we repeat the Monte Carlo
experiments with additional Gaussian noise (σ= 10 −6) injected into the parametersAandB.
Figure 3 (and Appendix Fig. A3) overlays the mean contrasts and confidence intervals for the

4.4 Perturbation Direction
Both perturbation protocols (b onlyandantisym) exhibit the same qualitative scaling behavior
and detectability structure. While the sign and prefactor of the contrast may differ between proto-
cols, the persistence of smooth scaling and robustness suggests that the observed stiffness behavior
is intrinsic to the underlying three-way geometry rather than an artifact of a particular perturbation
direction in parameter space.
4.5 Drift Observable
The drift contrast defined in Section 3.2 remains close to zero across all tested regimes, including
antisymmetric perturbations and complex continuation. This indicates that the modulus of the
lock locations is strongly constrained by the reciprocal structure under the present definition.
This result does not undermine the stability findings above; rather, it suggests that the cur-
rent drift observable is insufficiently sensitive to capture additional degrees of freedom (e.g. phase
variation in the complex plane). Refining this observable is a natural direction for future work.

Across deterministic scans, Monte Carlo ensembles, and robustness tests, the three-way lock struc-

5 Interpretation and Discussion
The numerical experiments reported in Section 4 provide a consistent picture of the behavior of
the three-way reciprocal construct. This section interprets those findings in the context of the
underlying geometry, while remaining explicit about what is and is not established.
5.1 Evidence for Structural Stiffness
The most robust empirical feature of the construct is the behavior of the stiffness contrast ∆ logS.
Across deterministic scans spanning more than one thousand orders of magnitude, as well as Monte
Carlo ensembles with and without injected noise, this observable exhibits:
•smooth power-law scaling in the perturbation magnitude,
•reproducible detectability thresholds,
•stability across numerical precision regimes,
•insensitivity to perturbation direction in parameter space.
Taken together, these properties are difficult to reconcile with the hypothesis that the observed
behavior is merely a numerical artifact. Instead, they suggest that the three-way intersection
conditionf(x) =±1 imposes a genuine structural constraint on the geometry of the rational
family, giving rise to a form of intrinsic stiffness.
Importantly, this stiffness does not imply rigidity in an absolute sense. The lock locations do
respond continuously to parameter perturbations, but they do so in a controlled, analytic manner
rather than exhibiting chaotic or unstable behavior.
5.2 Structure Versus Choice of Constants
Although the experiments are presented primarily for the constant pair (π, e), the qualitative
behavior does not appear to depend on this specific choice. Exploratory tests with integer constants,
algebraic constants, and other transcendental pairs show similar scaling and robustness.
This supports an interpretation in which the defining feature is not the arithmetic nature of the
constants themselves, but rather the structural properties of the reciprocal rational family. The
constants serve to parametrize the family, but do not appear to determine whether the three-way
lock exists or whether it exhibits stability.
5.3 Interpretation of the Three-Way Lock
The three-way intersection condition arises purely from the reciprocal structure:
f(x) = 1
f(x) ⇐ ⇒f(x) =±1.
This condition does not correspond to a fixed point of iteration, nor does it rely on any external
dynamical interpretation. Instead, it identifies points in the domain where the forward map, its
reciprocal, and the associated level sets coincide.
The empirical results suggest that these points behave more like intrinsic geometric features
than like accidental algebraic coincidences. In this sense, the three-way lock may be interpreted as
a structural invariant of the family under small perturbations, even though its precise numerical
location depends continuously on the parameters.

5.4 Limitations of the Drift Observable
One of the original motivations for this investigation was to explore whether the three-way structure
might exhibit multiple independent modes of response under perturbation. While the stiffness
observable clearly reveals meaningful structure, the current drift observable (based on deviation
from unit modulus) remains near zero across all tested regimes.
This should not be interpreted as a failure of the construct. Rather, it indicates that the
modulus of the lock locations is itself strongly constrained by the reciprocal geometry. More
sensitive observables — for example those based on phase variation in the complex plane or on
geometric distance inC— may be required to probe additional degrees of freedom.
5.5 What Is (and Is Not) Claimed
It is important to clarify the scope of these findings. The present work demonstrates that:
•the three-way reciprocal construct exhibits reproducible numerical stability,
•this behavior persists across precision regimes and perturbation schemes,
•the observed scaling is consistent with smooth analytic dependence.
The results donotestablish a closed-form analytical theory, do not assert physical interpre-
tation, and do not claim universality across all possible rational families. Instead, they provide
evidence that the construct studied here is mathematically nontrivial and warrants further analyt-
ical investigation.
5.6 Implications
From a methodological perspective, the work demonstrates that carefully designed numerical ex-
periments — incorporating controls, contrasts, ensemble statistics, and precision checks — can
serve as a meaningful exploratory tool in the study of novel geometric structures.
From a conceptual perspective, the results suggest that reciprocal three-way intersections may
represent a class of geometric features that are structurally stable rather than accidental. Whether
this phenomenon admits a deeper theoretical characterization remains an open question, and one
that motivates further work.
6 Conclusion
We have presented a controlled numerical investigation of a minimal reciprocal rational construct
exhibiting intrinsic three-way intersection points defined by the conditionf(x) =±1. Rather than
treating these points as abstract algebraic curiosities, we probed their behavior under systematic
perturbation using a rigorous experimental pipeline including deterministic scans, Monte Carlo
ensembles, robustness tests under noise, and dual-precision computation.
Across all tested regimes, the three-way lock points exhibit smooth sensitivity scaling, repro-
ducible detectability thresholds, and strong robustness to perturbation. These features are difficult
to reconcile with the interpretation of the construct as a fragile numerical coincidence. Instead, the
results support the view that the three-way intersection behaves as a genuine structural feature of
the rational family, with stability properties that persist across scales and perturbation protocols.
At the same time, the present work remains exploratory. No closed-form analytical theory is
claimed, and the observed scaling behavior invites further mathematical explanation. The drift

7 Limitations and Future Work
While the results presented here demonstrate consistent and reproducible behavior, the scope of this
study is intentionally limited. Several important directions remain open for further investigation.
7.1 Scope of the Construct
This work focuses on a specific quartic-over-quartic reciprocal rational family. Although exploratory
tests with alternative constants and minor structural variations suggest that the observed behavior
is not unique to the particular parameter choices used here, no claim is made regarding universality
across broader classes of rational maps. A natural extension would be to study whether similar
three-way stability properties arise in higher-degree families or in structurally distinct reciprocal
constructions.
7.2 Observables and Sensitivity
The primary observable used in this work, the stiffness contrast ∆ logS, proved highly informative
and robust. By contrast, the drift observable based on deviation from unit modulus did not exhibit
meaningful variation under the tested protocols. This suggests that the current drift definition may
be insufficiently sensitive to capture additional geometric degrees of freedom.
Future work may benefit from introducing alternative observables, such as:
•phase-based measures in the complex plane (e.g. differences in arg(x)),
•complex-plane distance metrics between corresponding lock points,
•curvature or higher-order geometric descriptors of the local lock neighborhood.
Such extensions may reveal structure that is not accessible through modulus-based diagnostics
alone.
7.3 Analytical Characterization
The present study is deliberately numerical and exploratory. Although the observed power-law scal-
ing and stability properties strongly suggest underlying analytic structure, no theoretical derivation
of these properties has yet been established. A rigorous analytical treatment of the scaling exponent,
the stability of the lock solutions, and the geometry of the reciprocal constraint would significantly
deepen understanding of the construct.

7.4 Broader Experimental Exploration
Only a limited subset of constant pairs and perturbation protocols were explored in detail. While
the qualitative behavior appears stable across tested cases, a more systematic survey of parameter
space may reveal regimes with qualitatively different behavior. Automated scanning across families
of constants or across generalized functional forms represents a natural continuation of this work.
7.5 Summary
These limitations do not undermine the current findings; rather, they clarify that the present study
should be understood as an initial, structured exploration of a novel geometric construct. The
numerical evidence establishes that the system is nontrivial, stable, and worthy of deeper math-
ematical investigation. Future work will determine how far these properties extend and whether
they admit a comprehensive theoretical framework.
