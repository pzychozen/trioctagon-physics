1 Introduction
Quantum mechanics and general relativity stand as two of the most successful yet fundamentally
incompatible theories in modern physics. While quantum mechanics accurately describes the
behavior of particles and fields at the smallest scales, general relativity governs the dynamics
of spacetime and gravitation at cosmic scales. The quest to reconcile these frameworks into
a consistent theory of quantum gravity remains one of the foremost challenges in theoretical
physics.
The original Three-Way Quantum Gravitational Wave Model introduced a novel approach
by postulating dual-planar interactions mediated through a Vesica Piscis wavefunction basis.
Although this preliminary formulation provided intriguing insights into inter-brane resonance
and structured quanta expansion, it suffered from several limitations, including the absence of
Planck-scale phase-shift corrections and unstable attractor dynamics.
In this work, we refine the Three-Way model by integrating Loop Quantum Gravity con-
straints, incorporating nonlinear self-interaction terms, and deriving a unified attractor equation
that stabilizes key physical parameters at±1. Our objectives are threefold:
1. To formulate a rigorous dual-planar gravity action that seamlessly unites brane-world
scenarios with Planck-scale corrections.
2. To analyze quantum coherence effects and phase-shift signatures relevant for LIGO/LISA
observations.
3. To establish a computational framework for numerical simulations and error analysis,
validating our model against existing gravitational wave data.
