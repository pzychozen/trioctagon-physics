Coherence Returns and Phase-Remainder
Bounds in the Native Tri-Octagon Map
Hilmir Frímann Halldórsson
Research draft v0.1 — for GPT/author review
31 October 2026
Abstract
The native Tri-Octagon map evolves three complex channel states through a nonlinear
prestage and a simultaneous harmonic-three phase update. We distinguish return of raw
coherence from return of the full state. On a single regular block, equality of raw coherence
is equivalent to a unit common-phase remainder; equivariance then propagates the block
without assuming global regularity. For one fixed rational coefficient tuple, we prove
absorption into a smaller amplitude domain, a sharper amplitude bound for every finite
unit-multiplier return, and a phase-sector exclusion. Independent review extends the
established 30-degree sector to the closed 60-degree sector: a regular finite return that
meets this sector must be a fixed state. A separate three-update chord estimate bounds the
principal remainder angle by approximately 18.5474 degrees and excludes the specific
quarter-phase remainder mechanism on the stated domain. The retained 60-start
investigation produced 42 numerical coherence-period-one outcomes in seven groups, 18
solver failures and no genuine period-three candidate. These finite numerical outcomes do
not exclude other dispersed-phase returns. Existing state and observer evolution remain
intact; a correspondence to the author's prescribed cone-pair choreography has not been
established.
Contribution in ordinary language
A repeated pattern can hide a common complex phase change. The paper identifies exactly
when that happens, limits how large a three-update phase change can be at the studied
settings, and shows that a finite return cannot sustain distinct stages inside a sufficiently
narrow phase sector. The numerical search supplied examples of simpler returns, not a
three-stage orbit. These are conclusions about the specified mathematical map, not
measurements of energy leaving a geometric opening.

later blocks repeat. At a zero prestage, assigning zero to its phase is part of the model, not a

10. Conclusions and bounded open questions
Four conclusions remain separate. First, the model already contains native state evolution
and geometric or memory-dependent observations. Second, a regular raw-coherence return
has an exact common-phase remainder, controlling repeated blocks. At the studied tuple,
finite unit-multiplier returns are amplitude bounded; any regular return meeting the closed
60-degree ordinary phase sector is fixed. A regular three-return also has the small
remainder-angle bound, excluding its quarter-phase specialization.
Third, the retained finite search found no genuine three-stage candidate. Simpler outcomes
and failures do not prove absence in the dispersed-phase domain. Fourth, no native-to-cone
evolution correspondence was established. The prescribed sequence remains a geometric
motivation, not a computed native motion or a disproved physical theory.
The open questions are correspondingly specific: whether a regular three-stage return
exists in the admitted dispersed-phase region; whether any numerical lower-period group
can be rigorously identified and certified; and which explicitly defined readout, if any, can
respect a desired pose evolution. These are open statements, not instructions for another
experiment. This draft fixes neither a phase order nor an observer clock to obtain a
preferred choreography.
Contribution and placement
The general criterion is an elementary symmetry consequence specialized to the regular
domain and zero convention. The series-specific deductions are the finite-block amplitude
bound, sector exclusion, remainder bound and combination with a complete retained
numerical account. The 60-degree extension is newly reviewed, with an independent proof.
No broad priority claim is made against external literature, which was not searched for this
task.
A focused mathematical research note with its evidence package is the proportionate
placement. Author and GPT review of claim wording, attribution, source placement and
unresolved interpretation remains appropriate before any public submission. Publication
decisions lie outside this task. The current artifact is a complete local review draft.
