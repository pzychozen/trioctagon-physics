AUTHOR CLARIFICATION

The intended top-view construction is:

1. Arrange three regular octagonal reference figures with C3 symmetry.

2. Each octagon contributes one relevant top edge.

3. Between neighbouring octagons are three triangular gaps.

4. Across each triangular gap, draw a straight connector segment between the corresponding endpoints of neighbouring octagon top edges.

5. The resulting six-edge top scaffold alternates:

   octagon edge
   gap connector
   octagon edge
   gap connector
   octagon edge
   gap connector

6. The octagons are scaled/positioned so that:

      octagon top-edge length
      =
      gap-connector length

7. When that equality holds, the six alternating segments form a regular hexagon.

The important object is the SIX-SEGMENT DERIVED GEOMETRIC STRUCTURE.

The octagons are only the scaffold used to define those six segments.

DO NOT interpret the central hexagon as necessarily being:
- a physical hole;
- bounded by filled octagon interiors;
- a material face;
- a welded mesh boundary;
- or a requirement that the octagons tile the plane.

The empty triangular gaps are allowed and are part of the reference construction.

PRECISE GEOMETRIC FORM

Let the three scaffold octagon edges be:

    E_A, E_B, E_C

and the three straight gap connectors be:

    G_AB, G_BC, G_CA.

The top closed chain is:

    E_A
    -> G_AB
    -> E_B
    -> G_BC
    -> E_C
    -> G_CA.

By C3 symmetry:

    |E_A| = |E_B| = |E_C| = s

and

    |G_AB| = |G_BC| = |G_CA| = g.

The untuned top structure is therefore an alternating equiangular hexagon:

    (s, g, s, g, s, g).

The design condition is:

    g = s.

At that condition the six sides are equal.

If the six turns are the intended 60-degree exterior turns
/ 120-degree interior angles, then the derived scaffold is a regular hexagon.

This should be proved explicitly from coordinates rather than assumed from a sketch.
