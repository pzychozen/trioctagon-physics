3 A common qualitative coeﬃcient neighborhood
Proposition 3.1 (Matched local domains) . There is a permutation-invariant neighborhood of
zero in Π on which the aligned branch and the two gauge-ﬁxed D1 branches are jointly deﬁned
for every h ∈ [0, 1/4], with regular state/prestage entries. The aligned proﬁle is independent of
h and
ri(ξ) = 1 + 2 5 ξi + O(∥ξ∥2). (3.1)
The claim is qualitative; it supplies neither a numerical coeﬃcient radius nor an attraction basin.
Proof. For positive aligned states all kicks vanish. Dividing the ﬁxed prestage equation by h
gives
Ei(r, ξ) = ri(1 + ξi − r2 i ) + 1 6 (L3r)i = 0. (3.2)
Its radius derivative at (1, 0) is −2I + L3/6, with eigenvalues −2, −5/2, −5/2. Analytic implicit
existence, positivity and local uniqueness follow. Uniqueness gives permutation equivariance.
Diﬀerentiating on Π, where L3dξ = −3dξ, gives ( 3.1). This specializes the inherited NRG
branch [ 1, thm:branch, eq:screen]. Equation ( 3.2), not the identity map alone, selects its
h-zero extension.
For D1, use its inherited seven-real-variable residual and border:
H(h, z, ξ, ν) = Fh(z; 1 + ξ) − eihνz h , (3.3)
det Bh = − (3h − 1)2 1600 , | det Bh| ≥ 1 25600 . (3.4)
The gauge is the seventh equation. The border retains all transverse state directions, including
unstable ones [ 2, D23–D27]. Near the compact reference interval, state and prestage compo-
nents have a common nonzero margin. The numerator of ( 3.3) is jointly analytic and vanishes
identically at h=0. Division of its convergent series by h gives the analytic extension Xk(z)−iνz .
To justify a common neighborhood, let R(h, y, ξ) be this extended seven-row system in displace-
ment variables, and A(h) = DyR(h, 0, 0). Its inverse is continuous and bounded on the compact
interval. A determinant lower bound is not itself a numerical inverse-norm estimate. Uniform
continuity on a ﬁxed regular box allows radii ρ, δ > 0 such that
∥I − A(h)−1DyR(h, y, ξ)∥ ≤ 1 2 , ∥A(h)−1R(h, 0, ξ)∥ ≤ ρ 2 (3.5)
for all h in the interval, ∥y∥ ≤ ρ and ∥ξ∥ < δ . The associated ﬁxed-point map is a contraction of
the closed radius- ρ ball into itself. Unique roots coincide with local analytic implicit branches,
so those branches glue. Regularity and invertibility persist slightly beyond the h endpoints.
Conjugation gives the second branch. Intersect with the aligned domain and shrink to an
invariant coeﬃcient ball.
This argument does not take an unjustiﬁed minimum of unrelated pointwise radii. At h > 0 the
aligned reference is normally stable: its amplitude multipliers are 1 − 2h, 1 − 5h/2, 1 − 5h/2, and
its noncommon phase multipliers are (1 − 3h/10)(1 − h/2). Their approach to one at h=0 gives
no uniform attraction assertion.
