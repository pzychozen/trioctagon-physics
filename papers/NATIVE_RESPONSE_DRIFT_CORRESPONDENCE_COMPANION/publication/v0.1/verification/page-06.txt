6 The local correspondence theorem
Theorem 6.1 (Local zero, sign and ratio correspondence) . There is a permutation-invariant
ball U about zero in Π, with one positive but unspeciﬁed radius for I = [0 , 1/4], such that the
selected branches satisfy
C = ASC, ν + = ASD, ν − = −ASD, S C, SD > 0 (6.1)
on I × U . Both factors are symmetric and real analytic on an open neighborhood of that set.
Their three common local zero lines are exactly
Z = {ξ ∈ U : ξ0 = ξ1} ∪ {ξ ∈ U : ξ1 = ξ2} ∪ {ξ ∈ U : ξ2 = ξ0}. (6.2)
Oﬀ Z, sgn C = sgn ν+ = sgn A, and ν− has the opposite sign. There is a symmetric positive
analytic function Θ across Z such that
ν± = ±Θ(h, ξ)C(h, ξ), Θ = SD/SC. (6.3)
For h>0 the same local zeros hold for raw C and both actual per-update advances. At h=0, raw C
and ω± vanish for every xi; the asserted zero set then applies only to the normalized or extended
quantities.
6.1 Analytic division before a ratio
The response factor is already analytic by ( 5.2)–(5.5). For drift, D1’s symmetries explain the
necessary division. Cyclic channel permutation preserves the ﬁxed-label rate. Conjugation
combined with a reﬂection preserves the reference label but reverses the rate, after carrying the
coeﬃcients and restoring the gauge. Local uniqueness gives alternation under odd coeﬃcient
permutations. The rate therefore vanishes whenever two coeﬃcients coincide [ 2, D28–D29].
An analytic function vanishing on a linear hyperplane is divisible by its deﬁning linear form:
take that form as one coordinate and factor its convergent Taylor series. After the ﬁrst division,
the quotient vanishes on a second hyperplane away from the intersection, where the ﬁrst factor is
nonzero. Continuity supplies vanishing at the intersection too, so a second division is legitimate.
Repeat for the third. On Π these sets are three distinct lines; h remains an analytic parame-
ter throughout. Uniqueness of analytic continuation makes the divisions agree on overlapping
neighborhoods.
Both the rate and A transform by the sign of a permutation, so the resulting quotient SD is
symmetric. This argument establishes a genuine analytic quotient at the intersections as well
as away from them. It does not divide a bare O(∥ξ∥5) remainder by A: near an equality line,
that cubic product can be arbitrarily small relative to ∥ξ∥3. Such an unqualiﬁed division would
not establish ( 5.8) uniformly near the zero set.
