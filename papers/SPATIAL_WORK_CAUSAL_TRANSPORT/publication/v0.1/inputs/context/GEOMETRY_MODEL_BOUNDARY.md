**Purpose:** Analytical boundary for the now-authorized constructive candidate. This note does not choose a physical connector, receiver, aperture, route or clock. W01–W05 remain accepted; a candidate realization is new proposed mathematics.

## 1. What the inherited delay actually means

The C6 observation uses
\[
\theta_X(s)=\frac{d_X(s)}{c_e},
\qquad
d_X(s)=\|X-\gamma_w(s)\|,
\]
with \(\gamma_w(s)\) on the retained carrier and \(X\) a fixed off-carrier observation point. The source explicitly assumes straight-line delay, \(1/d\) weighting and no loading. Here \(c_e>0\) is its independent observation parameter.

**This is Euclidean endpoint distance divided by an observation speed parameter. It is not the arclength or travel time of the intended outward–around–inward route.** The starting endpoint is a carrier point \(\gamma_w(s)\), not automatically the native core or central meeting region. The target \(X\in G\) is a point of an actual broken boundary, not a material receiver already identified by the source.

A mathematical delay channel of duration \(\theta_X(s)\) can reproduce this input/output timing while introducing a history state. If its internal coordinate ranges over \([0,\theta_X(s)]\), that coordinate initially measures delay or age. It is not a position in ambient three-dimensional space. Calling the new state a “line” does not establish a spatial line or curve.

## 2. The geometric cost of interpreting a delay channel as a route

Suppose a later physical interpretation supplies a rectifiable spatial path from \(\gamma_w(s)\) to \(X\), with arclength \(\ell_{X,s}\). Geometry then requires
\[
\ell_{X,s}\ge d_X(s).
\]
At constant speed \(c_e\), its travel time would be \(\ell_{X,s}/c_e\). Equality with the inherited delay \(d_X(s)/c_e\) requires a shortest path, hence a straight segment in unconstrained Euclidean space, up to an equivalent monotone parametrization. A nontrivial curved detour has greater length.

For a path-dependent speed, the time is instead
\[
T_{X,s}=\int_{\text{path}}\frac{d\ell}{v(\ell)}.
\]
Neither such a path nor such a speed law is supplied by C6. Retaining its numerical delay while later using a curved route could be a declared effective-model choice, but it would require an explicit explanation; it would not follow from the existing distance formula.

This distinction does not block an abstract causal/storage feasibility study. The study can retain \(\theta_X(s)\) as inherited mathematical delay data and leave spatial realization open. It must not claim that reproducing C6 timing has demonstrated the intended toroidal circulation.

## 3. What geometry and measures are already available

The accepted geometry supplies fixed labeled arcs \(G_{k,w}^{\sigma}\), a selected stationary curve \(\gamma_w\), their unit tangent and common registration, positive lower distance, a finite upper delay bound, and exact total G arclength. These are sufficient to define a family of abstract delay channels indexed by \((X,s)\), if that is the candidate's explicit new construction.

The measure \(ds\,d\ell_X\), with sums over the six G labels, is a mathematical product measure. A positive storage integral over it can be a legitimate model storage once its state and boundary balance are specified. It does not thereby become material energy density in space, and its normalization/units are not selected by the old observation formula.

In particular:

- G remains six two-segment broken arcs, with no added tip-to-tip chords, filled aperture surfaces, normals, centroids or tube thickness.
- An observation at a G point is not a crossing through an aperture or a load already attached there.
- An abstract channel bundle can carry multiplicity in the \((X,s)\) labels. A later spatial embedding must explain any shared paths, overlap, densities or double counting.
- The old receiver state \(z\) has no inherited material location. A new G-indexed state must not silently relocate it or count its existing storage twice.
- A positive history-storage realization and a causal receiving mechanism are different claims. The candidate needs actual port-work and boundary relations, not only an observable delay state.

## 4. Stationarity, width and direction conventions

Keep one fixed geometry configuration during each asserted result, with normalized \(\tau_g\in[0,h_g]\) and full reflected MINUS construction. Static shape data do not supply time evolution. Width scaling is common to geometry and carrier arclength; at fixed \(c_e\), the inherited delay scales with width.

The upper/lower labels on G and the old two port signs identify locations. They are not independently proved outward/inward flow signs. Opposite propagation directions in a delay channel require their own explicit state and port definitions. Reflection of a static hand is not time reversal.

Stationary native support lines remain stationary. Existing exact decoding about those lines does not itself attach transport channels to a material rod or to a G boundary. The carrier, intended tight toroid, optional tube, original close sphere and larger outer arrangements remain separate objects.

The construction need not decide between two co-present arrangements and rapid alternation unless it actually depends on that distinction. No current causal-memory proof requires a duty cycle or geometrical motion law.

Routine reversible choices of state space, symbolic parameters, boundary variables and proof organization can therefore be developed and challenged now. They should be presented as a candidate, not as deductions uniquely forced by C6 or an adopted physical theory.

- assigning the abstract delay channels to particular paths around the intended toroid;

- changing a material receiver/load interpretation or making a calibrated clock/speed claim.

If two concrete embeddings or attachments then differ materially, show their consequences and ask one focused question about the relevant distinction.

Do not force a choice between causal transport and C6 observation before the candidate has established the actual compatibility conditions. The abstract model's mathematical variables and the physical route's geometry can be assessed sequentially.
