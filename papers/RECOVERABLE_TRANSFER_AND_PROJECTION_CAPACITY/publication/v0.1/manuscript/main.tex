\begin{abstract}
The reciprocal chiral exchange candidate couples a native state to a periodic carrier and a reversible receiver through two finite distributed profiles. We distinguish the fraction of transferred storage that returns from the amount the fixed registration permits to leave the core. For arbitrary admitted preparations and every finite positive constant receiver coupling, the rank-two projection supplies a conserved, generally indefinite native scalar. Together with the published complex bilinear invariant, it yields a sharper all-time ceiling on signed net native debit. We determine the exact minimum on the full common native invariant level, characterize zero capacity by zero raw drive, and exhibit an active coherent launch for which the old ceiling is strictly unreachable. Algebraic sharpness does not imply dynamical attainment.

Separately, for the specified coherent family $\Omega_0=(1,e^{i\phi},1)$, $0<\phi<\pi/12$, with nonzero chiral drive and initially empty carrier and receiver, an explicit sufficient finite-coupling regime guarantees temporary receiver concentration, later receiver backflow and net native recovery on one unchanged solution. Receiver share and net recovery are at least $80/81$ of the stated intermediate transferred stock. The actual maximum stock on the prescribed window has a positive inverse-square leading scale in receiver coupling, while endpoint stock is bounded by a fourth inverse power. The proof controls propagation and native drift at finite coupling and separately bounds receiver power. High recovered fractions therefore coexist with small absolute transfer and necessary structural ceilings; neither a completed spatial circuit nor physical energy calibration follows.
\end{abstract}
